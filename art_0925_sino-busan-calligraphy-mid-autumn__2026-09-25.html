中秋再看筆墨 釜山華僑中學學生獲第六屆釜山學生書法節獎項

適逢中秋，本報再報導釜山華僑中學學生在第六屆釜山學生書法節獲獎消息。華僑家庭逢年過節以對聯與字畫布置家中的傳統，正透過學校書法教育延續。

釜山華僑中學透過學校公告宣布，在校生於第六屆釜山學生書法節多個組別獲獎。適逢中秋連假，本報再次梳理其意義。

Calligraphy for Mid-Autumn: Busan Chinese High School students win at 6th Busan Student Calligraphy Festival

For the Mid-Autumn Festival we revisit the awards won by Busan Overseas Chinese High School students at the 6th Busan Student Calligraphy Festival. The overseas Chinese household tradition of decorating homes with couplets and calligraphy at festivals lives on through school instruction.
By Jin Tae-heung · 2026.09.25

Busan Overseas Chinese High School announced that its students won awards in several categories at the 6th Busan Student Calligraphy Festival. With the Mid-Autumn holiday here, we look again at what it means.

광고 문의 · 300×250

In overseas Chinese families, festivals have always been close to the brush. Beyond Lunar New Year couplets, it has long been customary at Mid-Autumn to write verses about the moon and family to hang or give as gifts. School calligraphy lessons pass these everyday traditions to the next generation.

Literacy in every stroke

At Chinese schools calligraphy is more than an arts subject: learning stroke order and character structure by hand underpins Chinese reading and writing. Completing a piece within the time limit takes repeated practice and focus.

Winning at a festival where Busan students compete together is also a chance for Chinese school pupils to have their classroom skills recognised outside school and to mix with Korean students.

A line written together

Writing a short verse together at home over the holiday is one way to spend the festival. The school says it values the persistence students showed in preparing more than the awards themselves.

The full list of winners is available on the school website's notice board.
