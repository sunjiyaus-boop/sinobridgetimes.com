Lai’s campaign countered that the KMT was exploiting the issue to launch groundless attacks. It said Lai had long urged all sides to focus on positive policy visions and avoid negative labels that divide society, and noted that besides the watchdog’s recognition, Lai had been named an outstanding legislator 14 times by the Pocket Congress evaluation.

Concert economy spat spills into cross-strait records

The two candidates also clashed over the ‘concert economy,’ a signature policy of incumbent Mayor Chen Chi-mai. The United Daily News reported that the DPP camp resurfaced a video of Ko speaking in the Legislative Yuan about the band Mayday’s concert at Beijing’s Bird’s Nest stadium, and Lai said singers from free, democratic societies face heavy pressure when performing in China, accusing Ko of saying one thing and doing another. In a September interview, Ko had proposed a ‘Taiwanese Coachella’ festival in Kaohsiung’s Moon World landscape and a ‘concert economy 2.0’ with reasonable venue rents whose revenue would fund facilities such as restrooms and lighting.

In a rebuttal on Nov. 12, Ko’s campaign said Lai, as chief secretary of the city’s tourism bureau, attended the Straits Forum in China and met then-CPPCC chairman Jia Qinglin; as information bureau chief in 2012 he went to Beijing to seek more cross-strait direct flights; and as marine bureau chief in 2014 he led a delegation to Chinese agencies to attract cruise tourists. Noting that Lai in 2024 called KMT figures’ attendance at the forum a ‘united front showcase,’ the campaign said it was Lai who says one thing and does another. A further response from Lai’s camp could not be confirmed. With the Nov. 28 local elections just over two weeks away, the rhetoric in Kaohsiung, a traditional DPP stronghold, is growing sharper.

Many ethnic Chinese families in South Korea have roots in Kaohsiung and other parts of southern Taiwan, so interest in the race is high. Taiwan has no absentee voting, so voters holding Republic of China nationality must return home to cast a ballot. School and workplace bullying and using live performance to boost local economies are familiar issues in Korea as well, making Kaohsiung’s post-election policy direction worth watching.
