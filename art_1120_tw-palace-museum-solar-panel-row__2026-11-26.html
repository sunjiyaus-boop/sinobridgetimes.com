故宮 “제안한 적 없다”…能源署 “盤點은 강제가 아니다”

故宮은 즉각 반박했다. 연합보에 따르면 故宮은 이미 6월 10일 能源署에 문화재 신분 때문에 광전판을 설치하지 않겠다고 회신했으며, 11월 12일 서한은 能源署에 답하기 위해 문화재 주관기관인 타이베이시의 의견을 구한 절차였을 뿐이라고 밝혔다. 故宮은 본관 원구에 태양광 설치를 계획하거나 제안한 적이 없다며 蔡 국장에게 게시물과 AI 이미지를 삭제하고 공개 사과할 것을 요구했다. 이에 蔡 국장은 “사실을 말했는데 무엇을 사과하느냐”며 ‘본관’이 곧 역사건축물이고 ‘원구’가 곧 문화경관인데 ‘제안한 적 없다’는 것은 말장난이라고 맞받았다.

경제부 能源署도 중앙통신사를 통해 입장을 냈다. 能源署는 5월 27일 각 부처에 건물별 지붕 면적과 태양광 설치 현황을 확인해 달라고 요청했을 뿐이며, 문화재·노후 건물·정비 계획·안전 우려·지붕 면적 과소 등은 일괄 제외 대상이라고 설명했다. 能源署는 “盤點은 강제가 아니고 조사는 제안이 아니다”라며 공문을 왜곡하지 말라고 했고, 고궁 지붕에 패널을 합성한 AI 이미지는 오해를 부르고 故宮의 이미지를 해친다고 지적했다. 아울러 지붕형 태양광 설비가 10.51GW로 전체 태양광 용량의 약 65%를 차지한다고 덧붙였다.

能源署는 타이베이시 관내 문화재 두 곳에 이미 태양광이 설치된 사례도 들었다. 국가 고적인 龍山寺는 2025년 4월 28일 행정동 외벽에 플렉시블 태양광 패널과 저장장치를 설치했고 당시 蔣萬安 시장이 행사에 참석했으며, 시 지정 고적인 西門紅樓 ‘小八角樓’에도 2026년 5월 천공 없는 클램프 방식으로 태양광과 저장설비가 들어섰다는 것이다. 문화재라도 방식과 위치에 따라 설치가 가능하다는 취지로, 문화국의 ‘불가’ 논리와 정면으로 엇갈린다.

남부에서는 烏山頭 저수지 수상태양광을 둘러싼 공방이 이어졌다. 중국시보에 따르면 민진당 소속 黃偉哲 타이난시장은 18일 이 사업이 馬英九 정부 때 ‘승인’됐다고 했다가 국민당 蕭旭岑 부주석이 반박하자, 19일 “자료를 꼼꼼히 보지 못했다”며 ‘연구 검토(研議)’를 ‘승인(核准)’으로 잘못 읽었다고 인정했다. 다만 타이난시는 2015년 중앙정부가 저수지 태양광을 검토하기 시작했고 2016년 3월 수리서 공문으로 입찰 준비가 지시됐다고 설명했으며, 黃 시장은 韓國瑜 가오슝시장 시절 阿公店 저수지 태양광이 승인된 점을 들어 국민당의 ‘이중잣대’를 지적했다. 그는 2기 사업은 추진하지 않도록 했다고 밝혔다.

本報記者 楊念慈 · 2026.11.26

據自由時報、NOWnews等報導，台北市文化局長蔡詩萍19日在臉書發文，主旨為文化局堅守專業、不讓光電板破壞故宮意象，並附上AI生成圖。她公布故宮12日致文化局的書函，內容是函詢在歷史建築「國立故宮博物院」及文化景觀「國立故宮博物院院區」屋頂安裝太陽光電設備的妥適性。書函第三點提到，因能源署要釐清可裝設屋頂面積，若無法設置須具體填寫原因。

能源署並舉出台北市已有兩處古蹟設置光電的例子。國定古蹟龍山寺於2025年4月28日在行政大樓外牆設置可撓式太陽能板與儲能系統，市長蔣萬安當時曾出席；市定古蹟西門紅樓「小八角樓」則於2026年5月以免鑽孔夾具方式增設光電與儲能。意指文資依方式與位置仍可設置，與文化局「不可以」的論點正面交鋒。

南部的烏山頭水庫光電爭議也持續交鋒。據中時新聞網報導，民進黨籍台南市長黃偉哲18日稱該案是馬英九政府任內「核准」，遭國民黨副主席蕭旭岑反駁後，19日坦承「資料沒看仔細」，把「研議」看成「核准」，自嘲要點眼藥水。不過台南市府指出，中央於民國104年開始研議水庫光電，105年3月水利署公文已要求辦理招商；黃偉哲並點名韓國瑜任高雄市長時核准阿公店水庫光電，批藍營雙標，並表示已要求不再鋪設第二期。

The row extends the pre-election solar debate to cultural heritage.
By Yang Nyeom-ja · 2026.11.26

According to the Liberty Times and NOWnews, Taipei Department of Cultural Affairs Commissioner Tsai Shih-ping posted on Facebook on Thursday, along with an AI-generated image, that her department had stood firm to keep solar panels from spoiling the museum’s image. She released a Nov. 12 letter from the museum asking whether it would be appropriate to install solar equipment on the main building, a listed historic building, and on the museum compound, a designated cultural landscape. Point three of the letter said the Energy Administration needed to determine usable roof area and asked for specific reasons if installation was not possible.

The China Times reported that the department replied with a clear ‘no’. Tsai cited Articles 24 and 30 of the Cultural Heritage Preservation Act, saying preservation, restoration and reuse must keep a site’s original form and facade, and that rooftop solar would alter its appearance, skyline and landscape. She asked whether visitors ever see solar panels on the roof of the Louvre and criticised the central government’s energy policy.

Museum: no proposal; Energy Administration: survey is not coercion

The museum hit back. According to the United Daily News, it said it had already told the Energy Administration on June 10 that it would not install panels because of its heritage status, and that the Nov. 12 letter merely sought the city heritage authority’s view in order to reply. It said it had never planned or proposed solar panels for the main compound and demanded that Tsai delete the post and AI image and apologise publicly. Tsai replied that she had only told the truth, arguing that ‘main building’ and ‘compound’ are precisely the historic building and the cultural landscape.

The Energy Administration also responded via the Central News Agency. It said its May 27 letter only asked agencies to confirm each building’s roof area and existing solar installations, and that cultural heritage sites, aging buildings, sites under renovation, safety concerns and small roofs are all excluded. ‘A survey is not coercion and an inquiry is not a proposal,’ it said, urging people not to distort the document and calling the AI image misleading and harmful to the museum. It added that rooftop solar totals 10.51 GW, about 65 percent of national solar capacity.

The agency also pointed to two Taipei monuments that already carry solar equipment. Longshan Temple, a national monument, installed flexible panels and storage on the exterior of its administration building on April 28, 2025, at an event attended by Mayor Chiang Wan-an, while the city-listed ‘Small Octagonal Tower’ of the Red House in Ximending added solar and storage in May 2026 using a non-drilling clamp method. The point was that heritage sites can host panels depending on method and location, directly contradicting the department’s position.

Pre-election solar dispute widens to Wushantou Reservoir

Taipei Mayor Chiang Wan-an said the city government would firmly back its heritage staff. KMT legislator Wang Hung-wei and others cited the documents to support Tsai, while DPP legislator Wu Szu-yao called it a ‘fake issue’. On the same day, KMT mayoral candidates Chiang and Lee Szu-chuan reiterated their opposition to solar panels at Feitsui Reservoir.

In the south, the dispute over floating solar at Wushantou Reservoir continued. The China Times reported that Tainan Mayor Huang Wei-cher of the DPP said on Wednesday that the project had been ‘approved’ under the Ma Ying-jeou administration, but after KMT vice chairman Hsiao Hsu-tsen disputed this, Huang admitted on Thursday that he had misread ‘under study’ as ‘approved’. The city government still said the central government began studying reservoir solar in 2015 and a Water Resources Agency letter in March 2016 ordered tendering; Huang also noted that Akungtien Reservoir solar was approved while Han Kuo-yu was Kaohsiung mayor, accusing the KMT of double standards, and said a second phase would not proceed.

With local elections on Nov. 28, solar power has become a flagship issue in which parties clash over energy policy versus landscape and heritage protection. The gap between a central government pushing renewables and opposition-run local governments emphasising scenery and heritage was on full display in the museum row. For overseas Chinese in Korea and tourists who frequent the museum, the bigger question beyond the politics is how heritage standards will ultimately be clarified.
